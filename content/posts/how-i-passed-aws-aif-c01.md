### My Study Method
Honestly, my approach was mostly about drilling practice questions.

I would answer questions, take notes on concepts I didn't understand, and revisit the questions I got wrong. If I encountered an unfamiliar AWS service or AI concept, I'd look it up, understand the basics, and continue practicing.

I also skimmed through other people's study experiences on Reddit and elsewhere, but I eventually settled on the approach that worked best for me.

I didn't follow an elaborate study plan or spend hours reading every piece of documentation. Instead, I focused on identifying my weak points and repeatedly reviewing them.

Since I was working full-time, I studied whenever I had free time rather than following a strict schedule.

My biggest takeaway: Don't just memorize the answers. Make sure you understand why an answer is correct and why the other options are wrong.

### About the Exam

I took the exam in person at a test center in Shibuya, Tokyo, on September 12, 2026.

I chose the onsite option because I was worried about network issues during an online exam. I didn't want to deal with technical problems when I couldn't do much about them myself.

I didn't get an exceptionally high score, but I passed, and I'm happy with that, especially considering I was working full-time and had only about two weeks of effective preparation.

One memorable part of exam day: I was running late and literally ran for a good portion of the way to make it to the test center on time. Thankfully, the staff were kind enough to let me complete the exam.

Lesson learned: Don't underestimate travel time on exam day.
